The idea

Small fixes, not a full rebuild.

The concept is based on ordinary customer-facing problems: missing information, broken links, confusing contact routes and details that are difficult to use on a phone.

People cannot find the basics

Menus, services, prices, opening hours, locations or key details are hidden, outdated or difficult to read.

The next step is unclear

A call, booking, order or enquiry route is broken, buried too far down the page or awkward on mobile.

Public details do not match

A website, Google listing or social page shows different information, which can make a simple customer task harder than it should be.

Design constraint

Work with the setup a business already has.

The mock service assumes many small businesses already use Wix, Squarespace, WordPress, GoDaddy, Google Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram or third-party booking and menu links.

If someone else manages the website: the output could be clear notes rather than taking over the account.

If the business only uses social pages or Google: the public customer path can still be checked.

If access is complicated: the safest answer may be to involve the current provider instead.

Example customer flow

A short path from problem to agreed work.

1

Share a public link

No account access is needed for the first look.

2

Check the customer path

Can people find the basics and take the next step?

3

Agree a small scope

Make it clear what would change and what would stay untouched.

4

Show the result

Use screenshots and a short handover so the work can be checked.

Access and safety

Do not build the process around shared passwords.

The first check in the concept uses public links only. If a later fix needed access, the safer route would be proper platform permissions with the smallest useful role.

No passwords by form, email, social message or text.

Use limited access where possible: collaborator, editor or manager roles.

Avoid risky work outside the scope: payments, ordering systems and complex booking systems need more care than a small tidy-up.

Project decisions

Why the concept is scoped this way.

Why start with public links?

It keeps the first step low risk and lets the project focus on customer-facing problems before account access is even discussed.

Why no ranking or sales promises?

A small website tidy-up can make information clearer, but that is not the same as guaranteeing search rankings, sales or enquiries.

What if someone else manages the website?

The concept allows for handing over clear notes instead of assuming every client should give a new person access.

What sits outside the example scope?

Full rebuilds, SEO campaigns, paid ads, e-commerce, complex booking systems, branding and social-media management.
